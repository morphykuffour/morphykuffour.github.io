{%- comment -%} A run of stills off one clip, held and replaced rather than played: a stop motion. It began as the manta under the frieze and was made an include when two more clips wanted the same treatment, on the same reasoning the cascade was -- three copies of a reel and a stepped keyframe is three copies of one idea, and copies drift apart at the first fix. Parameters: dir folder under /images/ and the stem of the files in it: "manta" walks /images/manta/manta-01.jpg through -10.jpg count how many stills. Written into the markup as --frames and read back by the stylesheet for both the reel's width and its step count, so this number is the only place the length of the sequence is stated width the stills' own pixel dimensions, all of them the same. Required: height they reserve each frame's box, and the window's shape is handed to the stylesheet as the ratio between them, so a wrong pair is a box of the wrong shape rather than a hint pace how long one full pass takes. Divided by count, that is the hold on each still -- 2.5s over ten is a quarter second a frame, which is slow enough to read as stop motion rather than as footage. Three of these crossing want three different paces, which is the other half of what this parameter is for: in step they are one animation of three panels, out of step they are three sequences that happen to overlap phase optional, and negative: how far into its own pass the reel starts. Where the same clip is on the board twice, two copies at one phase are identical twins holding the same frame at the same moment; half a pass apart they are one sequence seen at two points in it, which is the trick the cascade upstairs plays with three copies of a video boomerang optional: set it and the reel runs the stills forward and then back again, which is what scripts/boomerang.sh does to a video file and for the same reason. A sequence that builds -- a drawing being drawn, a shape growing -- has its whole build wiped in one frame when the loop comes round, and that frame is the only cut in it. Run backward after forward and the end meets the beginning where it left it. The two turning frames are not repeated: the last still is not followed by itself, and the first is reached by the loop rather than written twice, which is the same pair of frames that script trims off ioda optional: set it and every still takes the theme's dark-mode opt-out, on the same terms the roll offers it. That is for ink on paper -- pencil lines want to be the light half of an inverted page -- and wrong for anything photographic, which wants its own colours back and gets them by taking the page invert twice label what the sequence shows, for anyone who cannot see it It emits a reel and nothing around it -- no heading, no caption, no section. Where one goes and what is said about it belong to the page, which is what lets three of them be laid over each other as one picture with one caption under it rather than three things stacked in a column. Stills rather than the clip they came from, which is what separates these from the cascades. Those want continuous footage -- the three-copy stack is a phase trick, and phase needs something running to be behind. These want the opposite.
